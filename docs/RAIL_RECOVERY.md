# Shallow floor-overlap recovery

The railstuck save in game/shuttleb restores a player hull intersecting world geometry. Sweeps return fraction zero, position remains fixed, and gravity keeps increasing velocity. The inherited CorrectAllSolid implementation synthesizes a contact but does not relocate the hull; the old steep-slope stuck response only injects velocity. A 1.75-unit lift clears this particular overlap.

The new single-player recovery runs only for an overlapping normal player hull moving downward or stationary. It probes up against gravity by at most two units. A candidate must be completely clear and a reverse sweep must meet an upward-facing world surface within 0.75 units of the original origin. It clears velocity accumulated while embedded and rebuilds contacts. Ground classification prefers an actual walkable contact at the feet over an average polluted by the rail bevels; wall walking and portal orientation transitions retain their existing handling. It does not search sideways, ignore colliders, enlarge portal exceptions, or escape deeper intersections. Spirit/deathwalk, vehicle and portal axis-transition paths are excluded. No save layout changes.

Validation uses the isolated railstuck save, muted hidden desktop tests, developer physics logging, deeper floor penetration and wall penetration fixtures, and the existing sloped portal-exit regression. The actual shipping 2006 executable was not compared, so the origin of the overlap is not established.

Follow-up validation holds the rail position for 20 samples with zero velocity, repeatedly jumps and lands back on the rail, and walks off under normal input. This addresses the earlier recovery-only implementation, which retained the accumulated escape impulse and could classify the rail top as a steep slope.
